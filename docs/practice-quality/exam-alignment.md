# Practice: design backward from the exam

Review original Practice only. Archived exam-question pages are reference evidence, not a place to publish changes. Do not copy exam text or use a different course's exam as sole evidence for an AA or AI authoring decision.

1. Identify an actual examined task for the exact course, level and paper (year, session, paper and part). Record the mathematical decision, command term, expected representation, technology and marks.
2. At Easier levels, isolate a necessary decision or calculation. Use the standard notation, preserve the command, and provide only the scaffolding the student needs. At Exam level, restore the ambiguity/method selection and linked steps that make the task exam-like. Marks and difficulty should follow the work, not length or unusual wording.
3. Review cross-course reuse separately. A shared syllabus statement is necessary but does not establish the style, expected depth, representations or calculator conditions. Keep transferable practice only where there is evidence, or explicitly exclude other courses with `notFor`. Test both the intended course and excluded courses using the actual Practice picker. Maintain course-native coverage when excluding an imported family.
4. Check multiple seeds, including negative or endpoint cases. No answer should be displayed in the question and no title should tell a student which technique to pick before they try it.

## First correction: AA SL 1.2 arithmetic sequences and series

- Exam demand: use arithmetic sequence/series notation and determine terms or finite sums. The AA course guide §SL 1.2 lists the (n)th term, sum formula and sigma notation. Exam bank `SL1.2.sum` reference points include May 2021 TZ2 P2 Q3(b), May 2023 TZ1 P1 Q8(a), and May 2025 TZ1 P2 Q8(c). The generated prompt is not a reproduction of those tasks.
- Learner report: the original Level 1 family asked for a mean between two “indices” and printed the answer as the middle term. The replacement gives the two endpoints in familiar notation and assesses counting inclusive terms and applying the sum formula. Seed 204980 now asks for the sum from (u_{19}) to (u_{29}), inclusive (–176), rather than the mean.
- Release scope: one AA-calibrated family at Level 1 in AA SL and AA HL. Other AI arithmetic-sequence warm-ups remain; this family is not syndicated to AI merely because §1.2 is common.
- Related audit: all 18 AI-authored families currently offered in AA SL were classified against their AA statements. The skills fit shared content; no broad exclusion was made. Follow-ups: an AI-authored graph-selection question offered at AA Level 4 should ask students to produce a sketch or be clearly assessed as recognition; an otherwise legitimate time-above-a-threshold model has a more typical AA radian representation. Review analogues at other levels and all four courses before extending this pilot.

## Graph construction and calculator angle notation

- AA SL May 2021 TZ2 P2 Q2(a), AA HL May 2022 TZ1 P2 Q10(a), AI SL May 2021 TZ2 P2 Q5(c) and AI HL May 2021 TZ2 P2 Q3(c) are classified in the existing course tags as producing a sketch from a graph or function. The student-produced output is distinct from recognising a picture. The original cubic practice now asks students in each course to sketch on paper and label the axes. The native self-mark step reveals a model graph and criteria only after checking; the intercept, extremum and count are still marked automatically. The paper request identifier and Level 4 are unchanged. Both current IB course guides list this under SL 2.3.
- AA SL May 2021 TZ1 P2 Q4 supplies a periodic-model reference and AA HL November 2021 P2 Q9(f) is classified as a GDC threshold-duration decision. AI SL November 2021 P2 Q3(e) and AI HL November 2021 P2 Q2(e) supply independently classified degree-model examples. For this existing Level 4 practice, AA now sees $\sin(\pi t/6)$ in radian mode and AI keeps the equivalent $\sin(30t^\circ)$ in degree mode. Both ask students to distinguish the two intersection times from the length of the interval. This is a course-specific authoring choice, not a rule that an AI paper can never use radians or that AA can never use degrees. The generated contexts and values remain original.
