Start from a two-by-two matrix. We will use its characteristic equation, not an eigenvalue menu.
Add the main diagonal entries for the trace.
For the determinant, subtract the product off the diagonal from the product on it.
The characteristic equation is lambda squared minus seven lambda plus ten. Find its discriminant.
For the square root, press Control and the x squared key. Enter nine, then press Right to leave the slot.
The discriminant is nine. Take its square root, three, and subtract it from the trace before dividing by two.
Use plus three instead. The matching upper root is five.
For lambda equals five, the first row of the eigenvector equation gives y equals x. Set x equal to one; use the vector with entries one, one.
The second entry is also five, so A times the vector gives five, five, or five times the whole vector. Any nonzero multiple of the eigenvector works.
For lambda equals two, the first row gives y equals negative two x. For the vector with entries one, negative two, the product gives two, negative four.
Five has the larger magnitude. Its direction dominates only if the starting vector has a component there.
