Solve two x plus y equals seven, and x minus y equals two. Use x, y in that order. Store the coefficients as A using Control and Template for the assignment symbol.
On a Calculator page choose Menu then seven: Matrix and Vector then one: Create then one: Matrix. Use two rows and two columns.
Enter two, one in the first row and one, minus one in the second. Tab moves to the next cell. Press Enter to store a.
Store b the same way, but use two rows and one column. Enter seven above two to match the original equation order.
Type A, then the power key, the negative key and one. Press Right to leave the exponent, then the times key, B, and Enter. The inverse of A multiplies B on the left.
Read x equals three above y equals one. In the original equations, twice three plus one is seven, and three minus one is two.
For x plus two y equals four, and two x plus four y equals nine, enter a two by three matrix, M. Keep x, y first; put the constants in the final column.
Open Menu, then seven for Matrix and Vector, then five for Reduced Row-Echelon Form. Enter M as the argument and press Enter.
The last row means zero times x plus zero times y equals one, so there is no solution. A zero row instead leaves one variable free here. For these cases, the inverse method does not apply.
