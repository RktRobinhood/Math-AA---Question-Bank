Estimate the mean of a normal population. The sample mean is forty and the sample size is thirty-six. First suppose we know the population standard deviation is six.
On a Calculator page, choose Menu then Statistics then Confidence Intervals then z Interval. Select Stats rather than Data.
Enter six for the population standard deviation, forty for the sample mean, thirty-six for the sample size, and zero point nine five for C Level. Enter a proportion, not ninety-five.
Read the lower and upper confidence bounds: about thirty-eight point zero four and forty-one point nine six. The margin of error is half the width, not an endpoint.
Now suppose six is only the sample standard deviation. Use t Interval with Stats. The distinction is whether the population standard deviation is known, not whether the sample size exceeds thirty.
For t, enter the sample mean first and the sample standard deviation second. Keep the sample size at thirty-six and C Level at zero point nine five.
About thirty-seven point nine seven to forty-two point zero three for the population mean. Over many repeated samples, this method would capture the true mean about ninety-five percent of the time.
