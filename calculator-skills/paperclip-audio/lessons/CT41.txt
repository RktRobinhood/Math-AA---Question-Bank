Ten percent is below twenty. The standard deviation is five. To find the mean, first match that percentile on the standard normal curve.
On a Calculator page, press Menu. Choose Probability, then Distributions, then Inverse Normal.
Enter zero point one for the left area, zero for the mean, and one for the standard deviation.
Confirm, then press Enter again. The standard normal z-score is negative. Keep the calculator’s stored value.
The mean is twenty minus five times that z-score. Type twenty minus five times. Press Control, then the negative key, to insert the previous answer. Press Enter. The mean is about twenty-six point four.
Now ninety percent is below sixty-three, and the mean is fifty-two. We’re finding the standard deviation. Open Menu, Probability, Distributions, and Inverse Normal again.
Use the left area, zero point nine. Again enter zero for the mean and one for the standard deviation. Confirm, then press Enter again. This z-score is positive.
Open a bracket. Enter sixty-three minus fifty-two, then close the bracket. Divide by the previous answer: Control, then the negative key. Press Enter. The standard deviation is about eight point five eight. It’s positive, as a spread must be.
If the question gives the right tail, subtract its probability from one before using Inverse Normal. The Area field takes the area to the left.
