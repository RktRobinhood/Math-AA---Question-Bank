Enter the directed arrow counts. Rows are where a walk starts; columns are where it goes.
Two arrows go from A to B, so the entry in row A, column B is two. An absent arrow gives zero.
Open Matrix and Vector, then Create, then Matrix.
Set three rows and three columns. Fill each count, then press Tab.
From after the last cell, press Control and Var to insert the store arrow, type A, then Enter.
Type a raised to power two, then Enter. This uses matrix multiplication.
From A to C, read row A, column C: two. The two parallel arrows from A to B each allow a walk through B. Read the reverse entry: zero.
For exactly three edges, use a cubed instead. Do not add shorter lengths to it.
There is one three-edge walk: A to C, back to A, then to C. Repeated vertices are allowed in a walk.
