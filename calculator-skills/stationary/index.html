    What a derivative zero tells you

    The zero of f₂ gives an x-coordinate. Substitute into f₁ for y. A + to − change gives a local maximum; − to + gives a local minimum. For the cubics in this lesson, the same nonzero sign on both sides of an isolated derivative zero gives a stationary inflection.

    A crossing search may miss a touching zero. Use a known candidate and verify it, rather than treating a near-zero number as proof. The bound fields and candidate panel are lesson helpers, not TI menus. The simulator accepts smooth polynomials up to degree three and models the non-CAS menus.
