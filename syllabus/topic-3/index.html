Topic 3: Geometry and Trigonometry

Prior Learning (Assumed)

Area

A_{\text{triangle}}=\frac{1}{2}bh
A_{\text{parallelogram}}=bh
A_{\text{trapezoid}}=\frac{1}{2}(a+b)h
A_{\text{circle}}=\pi r^2

Volume

V_{\text{cuboid}}=lwh
V_{\text{cylinder}}=\pi r^2h
V_{\text{prism}}=Ah

3.1 3D space, volume, angles, distance and midpoints

  • Distance and midpoint in three-dimensional space.
  • Volume and surface area of 3D solids, including combinations of solids.
  • Angles between intersecting lines or between a line and a plane.

3.2 2D and 3D trigonometry, sine rule, cosine rule and area

Sine rule

\frac{a}{\sin A}=\frac{b}{\sin B}=\frac{c}{\sin C}

Cosine rule

c^2=a^2+b^2-2ab\cos C

Area of a triangle

A=\frac{1}{2}ab\sin C

3.3 Applications of trigonometry

  • Applications of right- and non-right-angled trigonometry.
  • Pythagoras' theorem in context.
  • Angles of elevation and depression.
  • Bearings.

3.4 Circle: radians, arcs and sectors

Arc length

l=r\theta

Sector area

A=\frac{1}{2}r^2\theta

3.5 Unit circle definitions, exact trig ratios and ambiguous case of sine rule

  • Definitions of \sin\theta, \cos\theta and \tan\theta using the unit circle.
  • Exact trigonometric ratios.
  • Ambiguous case of the sine rule.

3.6 Pythagorean identity and double angles

\sin^2\theta+\cos^2\theta=1
\sin2\theta=2\sin\theta\cos\theta
\cos2\theta=\cos^2\theta-\sin^2\theta

3.7 Circular functions: graphs, composites and transformations

  • Graphs of \sin x, \cos x and \tan x.
  • Amplitude and periodicity.
  • Composite circular functions such as
f(x)=a\sin(b(x+c))+d
  • Real-life periodic models.

3.8 Solving trigonometric equations

  • Solving trigonometric equations in a finite interval, graphically and analytically.
  • Equations leading to quadratic equations in \sin x, \cos x or \tan x.

Additional higher level

  • 3.9 Reciprocal trigonometric ratios, Pythagorean identities and inverse trigonometric functions
    Defines sec θ, cosec θ and cot θ as the reciprocals of cos θ, sin θ and tan θ, and adds the Pythagorean identities 1 + tan²θ = sec²θ and 1 + cot²θ = cosec²θ. Covers arcsin x, arccos x and arctan x as functions: their restricted domains, principal-value ranges and graphs.
  • 3.10 Compound angle identities
    Compound angle identities for sin(A ± B), cos(A ± B) and tan(A ± B), and the double angle identity for tan. Includes deriving the double angle identities from the compound ones, with a link to De Moivre's theorem.
  • 3.11 Relationships between trigonometric functions and the symmetry of their graphs
    Relationships between the trigonometric functions shown by the symmetries of their graphs and the unit circle, such as sin(π − θ) = sin θ, cos(π − θ) = −cos θ and tan(π − θ) = −tan θ. Links to odd and even functions and to compound angles.
  • 3.12 Vectors: concepts, components and algebra
    Vectors as directed line segments, written in column form or with base vectors i, j, k. Position and displacement vectors (AB = b − a), sums, differences, scalar multiples, the zero vector, parallel vectors, magnitude, unit vectors and the distance between points, treated both algebraically and geometrically, plus proofs of geometric properties with vectors.
  • 3.13 The scalar product and the angle between vectors
    The scalar (dot) product, both in components and as v · w = |v||w| cos θ, and its algebraic properties. Using it to find the angle between vectors and to test for perpendicular vectors (v · w = 0) and parallel vectors (|v · w| = |v||w|).
  • 3.14 Vector equation of a line in two and three dimensions
    The vector equation of a line r = a + λb in 2D and 3D, the meaning of the position vector a and direction vector b, and its parametric and Cartesian forms. The angle between two lines from their direction vectors, and simple kinematics with λ as time, b as velocity and |b| as speed.
  • 3.15 Coincident, parallel, intersecting and skew lines
    Deciding whether two lines are coincident, parallel, intersecting or skew (non-parallel and non-intersecting, possible only in 3D), and finding the point of intersection when there is one.
  • 3.16 The vector product
    The vector (cross) product in components and as v × w = |v||w| sin θ n with n given by the right-hand rule, and its properties: anticommutative, distributive, v × v = 0, and zero for non-zero vectors exactly when they are parallel. Using |v × w| for the area of a parallelogram or triangle.
  • 3.17 Vector and Cartesian equations of a plane
    Equations of a plane in vector form r = a + λb + μc (b, c non-parallel vectors in the plane), scalar product form r · n = a · n with normal n, and Cartesian form ax + by + cz = d.
  • 3.18 Intersections and angles between lines and planes
    Intersections of a line with a plane, of two planes and of three planes, found by solving equations and interpreted geometrically (a point, a line, a plane, or no intersection), linking to systems of linear equations. The angle between a line and a plane and between two planes.

Topic 3: Geometry and Trigonometry

  • 3.1 3D space, volume, angles, distance and midpoints
    Distance and midpoint in 3D; volume and surface area of 3D solids; angle between two intersecting lines or a line and a plane.
  • 3.2 2D and 3D trigonometry, sine rule, cosine rule and area
    Right-angled trigonometry; sine rule; cosine rule; area = 1/2 ab sin C.
  • 3.3 Applications of trigonometry
    Angles of elevation and depression; bearings; constructing labelled diagrams from written statements.
  • 3.4 Arc length and sector area in degrees
    Arc length and sector area of a circle with the angle in degrees, as the fraction θ/360 of the circumference 2πr or the area πr². Radians are not used at SL.
  • 3.5 Equations of perpendicular bisectors
    Finding the equation of the perpendicular bisector of a segment, given either its two endpoints or the equation of the line containing it together with its midpoint. Uses midpoints, perpendicular gradients and the forms of a straight line from 2.1.
  • 3.6 Voronoi diagrams
    Voronoi diagrams: sites, edges, vertices and cells, and adding a new site. Uses include finding a boundary equation from given site coordinates, the nearest site to a point, cell areas, nearest neighbour interpolation and the 'toxic waste dump' problem, whose answer is at a vertex where three edges meet. Students do not construct bisectors geometrically.

Additional higher level

  • 3.7 Radian measure
    Definition of the radian and conversion between degrees and radians, with answers as exact multiples of π or decimals. Arc length l = rθ and sector area A = ½r²θ with θ in radians.
  • 3.8 The unit circle, the Pythagorean identity, the ambiguous case and graphical trigonometric equations
    cos θ and sin θ defined as coordinates on the unit circle, the graphs of sin x and cos x built from it, tan θ = sin θ / cos θ and the identity cos²θ + sin²θ = 1. The ambiguous case of the sine rule, and solving trigonometric equations graphically on a finite interval. Exact values are not assessed.
  • 3.9 Matrix transformations of the plane
    2 × 2 matrices for reflections, horizontal and vertical stretches, enlargements and rotations about the origin, with translations added as a vector, in the form Ax + b. Compositions of these, iterated transformations such as simple fractals, and the determinant as the area scale factor: image area = |det A| × object area.
  • 3.10 Vectors and scalars: representation and components
    Vectors and scalars, vectors as directed line segments and in component (column or i, j, k) form, the zero vector and −v. Sums, differences, scalar multiples and resultants, both algebraically and geometrically; parallel vectors; magnitude; position vectors; and rescaling to a unit vector or to a given magnitude.
  • 3.11 Vector equation of a line
    The vector equation of a line r = a + λb in two and three dimensions, where a is a point on the line and b a direction vector, and conversion to parametric form x = x₀ + λl, y = y₀ + λm, z = z₀ + λn.
  • 3.12 Vector kinematics
    Vectors in kinematics: constant-velocity motion r = r₀ + vt in two and three dimensions, positions and times, intersections and collisions, relative position AB and closest approach. Also variable velocity in two dimensions, with projectile and circular motion as special cases, and time shifts written with t − a.
  • 3.13 Scalar and vector products
    The scalar product v · w = |v||w| cos θ, the angle between two vectors, the acute angle between two lines and the perpendicularity test v · w = 0. The vector product v × w with direction by the right-hand rule, |v × w| as the area of a parallelogram or triangle, and the components of a parallel and perpendicular to b. Proofs and general properties are not required.
  • 3.14 Graph theory: vertices, edges, degree and types of graph
    Basic graph theory: vertices, edges, adjacency, degree; simple, complete, weighted and directed graphs with in-degree and out-degree; connected and strongly connected graphs; subgraphs and trees. Representing real structures such as maps, networks and circuits as graphs.
  • 3.15 Adjacency matrices, walks and transition matrices for graphs
    Adjacency matrices for graphs and directed graphs, walks and counting walks of length k (or up to k) using powers of the adjacency matrix, weighted adjacency tables, and transition matrices for strongly connected undirected or directed graphs, with Google PageRank as an application.
  • 3.16 Graph algorithms: Eulerian and Hamiltonian routes, spanning trees, Chinese postman and travelling salesman
    Walks, trails, paths, circuits and cycles; deciding whether Eulerian trails or circuits exist; Hamiltonian paths and cycles. Minimum spanning trees by Kruskal's and Prim's algorithms (including Prim's on a matrix). The Chinese postman problem with up to four odd vertices, with explanation of why it works; and the travelling salesman problem with a table of least distances, nearest-neighbour upper bounds and deleted-vertex lower bounds.
