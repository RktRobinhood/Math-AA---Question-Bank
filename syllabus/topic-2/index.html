Topic 2: Functions

2.1 Equations of straight lines, parallel and perpendicular

Forms of a line

y = mx + c
ax + by + d = 0
y-y_1=m(x-x_1)

Gradient between two points

m = \frac{y_2 - y_1}{x_2 - x_1}
  • Parallel lines have equal gradients.
  • Perpendicular lines have gradients whose product is -1.

2.2 Functions, notation, domain, range and inverse as reflection

  • Function notation, for example f(x), v(t), C(n).
  • Domain, range and graph.
  • Informal inverse function concept.
  • Inverse function as reflection in the line y=x.

2.3 Graphs of functions

  • The graph of a function and its equation y=f(x).
  • Sketching graphs from information given or from context.
  • Using technology to graph functions, including sums and differences.

2.4 Key features of graphs

  • Maximum and minimum values.
  • Intercepts, symmetry, vertex and zeros.
  • Vertical and horizontal asymptotes.
  • Points of intersection of curves or lines using technology.

2.5 Composite functions, identity and finding inverse

  • Composite functions.
(f \circ g)(x)=f(g(x))
  • Identity function.
  • Finding inverse functions.
(f \circ f^{-1})(x)=(f^{-1}\circ f)(x)=x

2.6 Quadratic function

Standard form

f(x)=ax^2+bx+c

Factorized form

f(x)=a(x-p)(x-q)

Vertex form

f(x)=a(x-h)^2+k
  • Includes graph, intercepts, axis of symmetry and vertex.

2.7 Solutions of quadratic equations and inequalities, discriminant and nature of roots

Quadratic formula

x=\frac{-b\pm\sqrt{b^2-4ac}}{2a}

Discriminant

\Delta=b^2-4ac

2.8 Reciprocal and simple rational functions, equations of asymptotes

  • Reciprocal function f(x)=\frac{1}{x}.
  • Rational functions of the form
f(x)=\frac{ax+b}{cx+d}
  • Vertical and horizontal asymptotes.

2.9 Exponential and logarithmic functions

  • Exponential functions and their graphs.
f(x)=a^x,\quad a>0
f(x)=e^x
  • Logarithmic functions and their graphs.
f(x)=\log_a x,\quad x>0
f(x)=\ln x,\quad x>0

2.10 Solving equations graphically and analytically

  • Solving equations using graphical and analytic methods.
  • Use of technology where there is no suitable analytic approach.
  • Interpreting roots and intersections of functions.

2.11 Transformations of graphs

  • Translations:
y=f(x)+b,\quad y=f(x-a)
  • Reflections:
y=-f(x),\quad y=f(-x)
  • Vertical stretch:
y=pf(x)
  • Horizontal stretch:
y=f(qx)

Additional higher level

  • 2.12 Polynomial functions: factor and remainder theorems, sum and product of roots
    Polynomial functions of any degree: their graphs, zeros, roots and factors; the factor and remainder theorems; and the sum (−aₙ₋₁/aₙ) and product ((−1)ⁿa₀/aₙ) of the roots of a polynomial equation, including equations with complex roots. Only the sum and the product of the roots are listed, not sums of pairwise products.
  • 2.13 Rational functions with a quadratic numerator or denominator
    Rational functions with a linear numerator over a quadratic denominator, and with a quadratic numerator over a linear denominator, with the reciprocal function as a special case. Sketches must show every asymptote (vertical, horizontal and oblique) and all axis intercepts.
  • 2.14 Odd and even functions, inverses with a restricted domain, self-inverse functions
    Odd functions (f(−x) = −f(x)) and even functions (f(−x) = f(x)), including periodic ones such as sin, cos and tan; finding an inverse function, restricting the domain where needed so that one exists; and self-inverse functions, where f⁻¹ = f.
  • 2.15 Solving inequalities g(x) ≥ f(x) graphically and analytically
    Solving inequalities of the form g(x) ≥ f(x) both from graphs and analytically. Algebraic or graphical methods are expected for simple polynomials up to degree 3; technology is used for these and for other functions.
  • 2.16 Graphs of |f(x)|, f(|x|), 1/f(x), f(ax + b) and [f(x)]²; modulus equations and inequalities
    Graphs of y = |f(x)|, y = f(|x|), y = 1/f(x), y = f(ax + b) and y = [f(x)]² drawn from the graph of y = f(x); and solving equations and inequalities that involve the modulus, algebraically for simple cases and with technology otherwise.

Topic 2: Functions

  • 2.1 Equations of straight lines, parallel and perpendicular
    Gradient; y = mx + c, ax + by + d = 0, y - y1 = m(x - x1); parallel and perpendicular lines.
  • 2.2 Functions, notation, domain, range and inverse as reflection
    Concept of function, domain, range and graph; function notation; inverse function as reflection in y = x; f^-1 notation.
  • 2.3 Graphs of functions
    Creating a sketch from information or context; using technology to graph functions including sums and differences.
  • 2.4 Key features of graphs
    Max/min, intercepts, symmetry, vertex, zeros, asymptotes; intersection of curves/lines using technology.
  • 2.5 Modelling with linear, quadratic, exponential, power, cubic and sinusoidal functions
    Using a family of standard functions as models of real situations: linear (including piecewise linear), quadratic (vertex, axis of symmetry, zeros and intercepts, with technology allowed for roots), exponential ka^x + c, ka^(−x) + c and ke^(rx) + c with their horizontal asymptote, direct and inverse variation ax^n (n ∈ ℤ, with the y-axis as an asymptote when n < 0), cubic, and sinusoidal a sin(bx) + d or a cos(bx) + d in degrees. For sinusoids, only amplitude a, period 360°/b and principal axis y = d are needed; converting between sin and cos is not required.
  • 2.6 The modelling process: choose, fit, test and use a model
    Following the modelling cycle with the 2.5 (and, at HL, 2.9) function families: choose a suitable model and sensible domain from a context, find its parameters from initial conditions, given points or simultaneous equations solved with technology (up to three linear equations in three unknowns at SL), judge and justify the model, and use it to predict while knowing the risks of extrapolation. Fitting by regression belongs to topic 4, and SL students do not perform non-linear regression.

Additional higher level

  • 2.7 Composite and inverse functions in context
    Composite functions (f ∘ g)(x) = f(g(x)) set in context, and inverse functions: finding f⁻¹, using f ∘ f⁻¹ = f⁻¹ ∘ f = identity, and restricting a domain (for example to one side of a quadratic's vertex) so an inverse exists.
  • 2.8 Transformations of graphs
    Translations, reflections in the axes, vertical and horizontal stretches; composite transformations.
  • 2.9 Further models: half-life, logarithmic, sinusoidal, logistic and piecewise
    Extending the SL model families with half-life from exponential models, natural logarithmic models f(x) = a + b ln x, sinusoidal models a sin(b(x − c)) + d in radians (period 2π/b, with c a phase shift), logistic models L/(1 + Ce^(−kx)) with carrying capacity L, and piecewise models, including finding a parameter that makes the pieces join. Students may also meet unfamiliar models defined in a question; the formal definition of continuity is not required.
  • 2.10 Logarithmic scales and linearising data
    Using logarithms to compress very large or very small numbers onto a manageable scale, and to linearise data: plotting log y against x (semi-log) tests for an exponential relationship and log y against log x (log-log) tests for a power relationship, with the best-fit line giving the parameters. Students interpret semi-log and log-log graphs but are not expected to draw or sketch them.
