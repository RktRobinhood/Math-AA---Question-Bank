Topic 5: Calculus

5.1 Introduction to calculus

  • Limits from a table or graph.
  • Derivative interpreted as a gradient function and as a rate of change.
  • Notation including \frac{dy}{dx}, f'(x) and related contextual notation.

5.2 Increasing and decreasing functions

  • Graphical interpretation of f'(x)>0, f'(x)=0 and f'(x)<0.
  • Intervals of increase and decrease.

5.3 Differentiating polynomials, n in Z

\frac{d}{dx}(ax^n)=anx^{n-1}, \quad n\in\mathbb{Z}
  • Differentiating sums and multiples of polynomial terms with integer exponents.

5.4 Tangents and normals

  • Tangents and normals at a given point.
  • Equations of tangents and normals.

5.5 Integration introduction, areas between curve and x-axis

  • Integration as anti-differentiation for polynomial functions with integer exponents.
  • Anti-differentiation with boundary conditions.
  • Definite integrals using technology.
  • Area under a curve where f(x)>0.

5.6 Differentiating polynomials n in Q, chain, product and quotient rules

\frac{d}{dx}(x^n)=nx^{n-1}, \quad n\in\mathbb{Q}
\frac{d}{dx}(\sin x)=\cos x
\frac{d}{dx}(\cos x)=-\sin x
\frac{d}{dx}(e^x)=e^x
\frac{d}{dx}(\ln x)=\frac{1}{x}

Chain rule

\frac{dy}{dx}=\frac{dy}{du}\frac{du}{dx}

Product rule

\frac{d}{dx}(uv)=u\frac{dv}{dx}+v\frac{du}{dx}

Quotient rule

\frac{d}{dx}\left(\frac{u}{v}\right)=\frac{v\frac{du}{dx}-u\frac{dv}{dx}}{v^2}

5.7 The second derivative

  • Second derivative notation including \frac{d^2y}{dx^2} and f''(x).
  • Graphical behaviour and relationships between f, f' and f''.

5.8 Optimization, local extrema and points of inflexion

  • Local maximum and minimum points.
  • Testing for maxima and minima using the first or second derivative.
  • Optimization problems.
  • Points of inflexion and concavity.

5.9 Kinematics problems

v=\frac{ds}{dt}
a=\frac{dv}{dt}=\frac{d^2s}{dt^2}
\text{displacement}=\int_{t_1}^{t_2}v(t)\,dt
\text{distance}=\int_{t_1}^{t_2}|v(t)|\,dt

5.10 Indefinite integration, reverse chain and substitution

\int x^n\,dx=\frac{x^{n+1}}{n+1}+C \quad (n\ne -1)
\int \frac{1}{x}\,dx=\ln x+C
\int e^x\,dx=e^x+C
  • Includes composites with linear functions.
  • Integration by inspection or reverse chain rule.

5.11 Definite integrals, areas under curve onto x-axis and areas between curves

  • Definite integrals, including analytical approaches.
\int_a^b g'(x)\,dx=g(b)-g(a)
  • Areas under curves, including when f(x) is positive or negative.
  • Areas between curves.

Additional higher level

  • 5.12 Continuity, differentiability, limits and differentiation from first principles
    An informal sense of continuity and differentiability at a point (never tested formally), limits as convergence or divergence, and the derivative defined from first principles as the limit of (f(x + h) − f(x))/h, used for polynomials only. Higher derivatives with the notations dⁿy/dxⁿ and f⁽ⁿ⁾(x), including nth-derivative patterns proved by induction.
  • 5.13 Limits by l'Hôpital's rule or Maclaurin series
    Limits of quotients f(x)/g(x) as x → a or x → ∞ that give the indeterminate forms 0/0 or ∞/∞, found with l'Hôpital's rule (applied more than once if needed) or by Maclaurin series. Includes the standard result sin θ/θ → 1 as θ → 0 and links to horizontal asymptotes.
  • 5.14 Implicit differentiation, related rates of change and optimisation
    Implicit differentiation of relations in x and y, related rates of change linked by the chain rule, and optimisation problems, including cases where the best value is at an endpoint of the domain rather than at a stationary point.
  • 5.15 Further standard derivatives and integrals
    Derivatives of tan x, sec x, cosec x, cot x, aˣ, logₐ x, arcsin x, arccos x and arctan x, the matching indefinite integrals (read as a family of curves), and the same results for composites with a linear function. Partial fractions (up to two distinct linear factors) are used to rearrange integrands.
  • 5.16 Integration by substitution and by parts
    Integration by substitution beyond the reverse chain rule (the substitution is given unless the integral has the form ∫ k g′(x) f(g(x)) dx), with changed limits for definite integrals. Integration by parts, including repeated use and the cyclic case such as ∫ eˣ sin x dx.
  • 5.17 Areas against the y-axis and volumes of revolution
    Areas between a curve and the y-axis over a y-interval, found by integrating x with respect to y. Volumes of solids formed by rotating a region through 2π about the x-axis (V = π∫ y² dx) or the y-axis (V = π∫ x² dy), including regions between two curves and design contexts.
  • 5.18 First-order differential equations
    First-order differential equations: numerical solution of dy/dx = f(x, y) by Euler's method with constant step h; exact solution of separable equations (including the logistic equation, using partial fractions), homogeneous equations dy/dx = f(y/x) via y = vx, and linear equations y′ + P(x)y = Q(x) via an integrating factor. Contexts include cooling, population growth and radioactive decay.
  • 5.19 Maclaurin series
    Maclaurin series for eˣ, sin x, cos x, ln(1 + x) and (1 + x)ᵖ with p rational, and new series built from these by substitution, multiplication, differentiation and integration. Series can also be developed from a differential equation by repeated differentiation.

Topic 5: Calculus

  • 5.1 Introduction to calculus
    Concept of a limit; derivative as gradient function and as rate of change.
  • 5.2 Increasing and decreasing functions
    Graphical interpretation of f'(x) > 0, = 0, < 0.
  • 5.3 Differentiating polynomials, n in Z
    Derivative of ax^n for integer n; derivatives of polynomial functions.
  • 5.4 Tangents and normals
    Tangents and normals at a given point, and their equations.
  • 5.5 Integration introduction, areas between curve and x-axis
    Anti-differentiation of ax^n (n integer, n != -1); boundary conditions to find c; definite integrals and areas above the x-axis using technology.
  • 5.6 Stationary points: local maxima and minima
    Finding where a curve has zero gradient by solving f′(x) = 0, with technology used to produce f′ and to solve, and identifying local maximum and minimum points. Students should know that a local maximum or minimum need not be the greatest or least value of the function on a given domain; the second derivative test is not part of SL (it is 5.10).
  • 5.7 Optimisation in context
    Solving optimisation problems in context with calculus and technology: maximising profit or volume, minimising cost or material, including setting up the function from a constraint such as a fixed volume or surface area. Kinematics optimisation is not set at SL.
  • 5.8 Approximating areas with the trapezoidal rule
    Estimating an area with the trapezoidal rule using intervals of equal width, from a function or from a table of data such as measurements of an irregular shape. Includes comparing the estimate with the true value and saying whether it is an over- or underestimate.

Additional higher level

  • 5.9 Further differentiation: standard derivatives, chain, product and quotient rules, related rates
    Differentiating sin x, cos x, tan x, eˣ, ln x and xⁿ for rational n, and combining them with the chain, product and quotient rules. Applying derivatives to related rates of change and, through the link to 5.6 and 5.7, to stationary points and optimisation.
  • 5.10 The second derivative and the second derivative test
    The second derivative in both notations, d²y/dx² and f″(x), and its use in the second derivative test for maxima and minima. Concave-up (f″ > 0) and concave-down (f″ < 0), and points of inflexion as points where the concavity changes, interpreted in context.
  • 5.11 Further integration: standard integrals, inspection and substitution
    Definite and indefinite integrals of xⁿ for rational n (including n = −1, giving ln x), sin x, cos x, 1/cos²x and eˣ. Integrating by inspection or by the substitution u = g(x) for integrals of the form ∫ f(g(x))g′(x) dx, including linear insides such as sin(2x + 5).
  • 5.12 Areas against either axis and volumes of revolution
    Areas of regions bounded by a curve and either the x-axis or the y-axis over a given interval, including parts below the axis where the integral is negative. Volumes of revolution about the x-axis (V = ∫ πy² dx) or the y-axis (V = ∫ πx² dy).
  • 5.13 Kinematics problems
    Displacement, velocity, acceleration; total distance travelled.
  • 5.14 Setting up differential equations and separating variables
    Forming a differential equation from a description in context, such as a rate proportional to the amount present, and solving it by separating the variables. Includes the general solution (with an arbitrary constant), particular solutions from given conditions, and the exponential model as the solution of dy/dx = ky.
  • 5.15 Slope fields
    Using and interpreting slope fields for dy/dx = f(x, y): drawing short segments with the gradient at grid points, matching fields to equations, sketching solution curves through a given point and reading features such as equilibrium solutions and long-term behaviour.
  • 5.16 Euler's method for single and coupled first-order equations
    Euler's method for approximate solutions of dy/dx = f(x, y) and of coupled systems dx/dt = f₁(x, y, t), dy/dt = f₂(x, y, t), such as predator-prey models. Values are generated with a spreadsheet or GDC; students should understand the step-by-step method, the effect of the step size and how to interpret the output.
  • 5.17 Phase portraits for coupled linear differential equations
    Phase portraits for dx/dt = ax + by, dy/dt = cx + dy with distinct non-zero eigenvalues: classifying the origin from the eigenvalues (real, complex or purely imaginary), sketching trajectories and identifying equilibrium points, stability and saddle points. Exact solutions are only needed for real distinct eigenvalues.
  • 5.18 Second-order differential equations by Euler's method
    Approximate solutions of second-order equations d²x/dt² = f(x, dx/dt, t) by rewriting them as the coupled system dx/dt = y, dy/dt = f(x, y, t) and applying Euler's method. Equations of the form d²x/dt² + a dx/dt + bx = 0 can also be studied with the phase portraits of 5.17; the equation is always given in examinations.
